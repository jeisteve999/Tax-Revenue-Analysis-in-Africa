# Tax-Revenue-Analysis-in-Africa
This project presents an interactive dashboard analyzing tax revenue as a percentage of GDP across OECD countries and African economies between 2015 and 2024.

Tax Revenue Analysis in Africa

This project presents an exploratory analysis of tax revenue trends across African countries using data from the IMF World Revenue Longitudinal Database (WoRLD), accessed via the OECD Data Explorer.

The dashboard focuses on understanding how governments in Africa generate revenue relative to the size of their economies (% of GDP), and how this compares to OECD benchmarks.

# Dashboard 1 – Tax Structure Evolution

![Dashboard ](https://github.com/jeisteve999/Tax-Revenue-Analysis-in-Africa/blob/main/dashboard.bi.png)

## Objectives

- Analyze the evolution of tax revenue in African countries over time  
- Compare Africa’s average tax performance against OECD countries  
- Identify top and bottom performers in terms of tax revenue (% of GDP)  
- Explore the distribution of tax revenue across countries  
- Highlight structural differences in fiscal capacity

## Key Insights

- African countries, on average, collect significantly less tax revenue (% of GDP) compared to OECD countries  
- There is considerable variation across countries, with some outperforming the OECD average  
- A large number of countries cluster in lower tax revenue ranges, indicating limited fiscal capacity  
- The gap between top and bottom performers highlights structural and institutional differences  


# Data Source

The dataset used in this project was obtained from the OECD Data Explorer:
